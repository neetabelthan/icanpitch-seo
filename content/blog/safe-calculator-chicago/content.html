What Is a SAFE and Why Chicago Investors Use Them

The Simple Agreement for Future Equity (SAFE) was created by Y Combinator in 2013 as an alternative to convertible notes. SAFEs represent the right to purchase equity in a future priced round, providing investors early-stage exposure without immediately setting a company valuation or creating debt obligations.

Core SAFE Mechanics

SAFEs convert to equity when a qualifying financing event occurs—typically a priced Series A round. The conversion happens at a discount to the Series A price, a capped valuation, or whichever mechanism provides the investor better terms. This structure aligns investor and founder interests: both parties benefit from maximizing the Series A valuation, since higher valuations validate the company's progress while SAFE investors still receive preferential conversion terms.

Unlike convertible notes, SAFEs have no maturity date and no interest rate. This eliminates the pressure of debt repayment and removes negotiation friction around note terms. For Chicago founders raising capital while building toward product-market fit, SAFEs provide crucial flexibility to focus on customer development rather than fundraising timelines.

Why Chicago Investors Favor SAFEs

Chicago's investment community has embraced SAFEs for several ecosystem-specific reasons:

  • Speed and Efficiency: Chicago investors value operational efficiency. SAFEs close faster than priced rounds (typically 2-4 weeks versus 8-12 weeks), allowing founders to return to building quickly
  • Deferred Valuation Discussions: Pre-seed and early seed-stage companies often lack the metrics for rigorous valuation analysis. SAFEs defer valuation debates until companies have revenue, user growth, or other objective metrics
  • Founder-Friendly Philosophy: Chicago's emerging investor class increasingly embraces founder-friendly terms, and SAFEs align with this trend by avoiding board seats, liquidation preferences, and other governance complexities in early stages
  • Standard Documentation: Y Combinator's open-source SAFE documents reduce legal costs—critical in a market where founders carefully manage burn rate

Understanding SAFE Terms: Valuation Caps, Discounts, and Pro Rata Rights

SAFEs contain several key terms that founders must understand to model dilution and negotiate effectively.

Valuation Cap

The valuation cap sets the maximum effective valuation at which the SAFE converts to equity. If your Series A occurs at a $20 million pre-money valuation but your SAFE has a $5 million cap, SAFE investors convert as if they invested at a $5 million valuation, receiving 4x more shares per dollar invested than Series A investors.

Chicago Norms:

  • Pre-seed: $3.5M-$6M caps
  • Seed: $8M-$12M caps
  • Caps typically 2-3x the current implied valuation, providing investors meaningful upside while founders retain majority ownership through Series A

Discount Rate

The discount rate gives SAFE investors a percentage discount to the Series A price per share. A 20% discount means SAFE investors pay 80% of the Series A price, receiving 25% more shares per dollar invested.

Chicago Norms:

  • Standard discount: 15-20%
  • MFN (Most Favored Nations) SAFEs without caps: 15-20% discount only
  • SAFEs with caps typically include 15-20% discount as additional downside protection for investors
  • Discounts above 20% are rare and signal potential concerns about company progress or competitive dynamics

How Caps and Discounts Interact

When a SAFE includes both a cap and discount, investors receive whichever terms are more favorable. This means:

  • High Series A Valuation (above cap): SAFE converts at the cap, discount becomes irrelevant
  • Series A Valuation Near Cap: SAFE likely converts at cap, though discount may provide slightly better terms
  • Series A Valuation Below Cap: Discount determines conversion (though this scenario is rare, as it suggests company regression)

Example: Chicago Seed-Stage Startup

Startup raises $1.5M on SAFE with $10M cap and 20% discount. Series A occurs at $25M pre-money valuation:

  • Cap conversion: $1.5M ÷ $10M = 15% ownership on fully diluted basis
  • Discount conversion: $1.5M at 20% discount to $25M = 7.5% ownership
  • SAFE converts at cap (more favorable), giving investors 15% of company

Pro Rata Rights

Pro rata rights give SAFE investors the option to invest in future rounds to maintain their ownership percentage. This term has become increasingly common in Chicago SAFEs, particularly for institutional investors.

Chicago Norms:

  • Institutional investors ($250K+ checks) typically negotiate pro rata rights
  • Angel investors and smaller checks ($25K-$100K) often lack pro rata rights
  • Some Chicago investors request "super pro rata" rights to invest beyond their ownership percentage in future rounds, though founders should resist this unless investor provides exceptional strategic value

Most Favored Nations (MFN) Clauses

MFN provisions allow early SAFE investors to adopt terms from later SAFEs if those terms are more favorable. This protects early investors from being diluted by sweetheart deals to later investors.

Chicago Considerations:

  • MFN clauses are standard in Chicago SAFEs
  • Founders should track all SAFE terms carefully to understand how MFN clauses might trigger
  • Avoid offering materially better terms to later investors unless company circumstances have significantly changed

How to Calculate SAFE Dilution: Step-by-Step Guide

Understanding dilution mechanics helps founders model cap table scenarios and make informed decisions about fundraising strategy.

Basic Dilution Calculation

Step 1: Determine Total Capital Raised on SAFEs

Sum all SAFE investments. Example: Raised $1.2M total across multiple SAFE investors, all with $10M cap and 20% discount.

Step 2: Identify Series A Terms

Series A occurs at $25M pre-money valuation, raising $7M at $32M post-money valuation.

Step 3: Calculate SAFE Conversion

SAFEs convert at cap ($10M) since Series A valuation ($25M) exceeds cap:

  • SAFE ownership = $1.2M ÷ $10M = 12% of company

Step 4: Calculate Series A Investor Ownership

Series A ownership = $7M ÷ $32M post-money = 21.875% of company

Step 5: Calculate Founder Ownership

Founder ownership = 100% - 12% (SAFE) - 21.875% (Series A) = 66.125%

Note: This simplified calculation excludes employee option pool (typically 10-15% at Series A), which would further dilute founders to approximately 56-59% ownership.

Multiple SAFE Tranches with Different Terms

Many Chicago startups raise SAFEs at different stages with varying caps. Example scenario:

  • Tranche 1: $500K at $4M cap (pre-seed)
  • Tranche 2: $800K at $8M cap (bridge to seed)
  • Tranche 3: $1.2M at $12M cap (seed)
  • Series A: $25M pre-money, raising $7M

Dilution Calculation:

  • Tranche 1 ownership: $500K ÷ $4M = 12.5%
  • Tranche 2 ownership: $800K ÷ $8M = 10.0%
  • Tranche 3 ownership: $1.2M ÷ $12M = 10.0%
  • Total SAFE ownership: 32.5% (substantial dilution)
  • Series A ownership: $7M ÷ post-money valuation

Post-money valuation calculation with multiple SAFE tranches becomes more complex and typically requires spreadsheet modeling or dedicated SAFE calculators.

Chicago Investor Expectations Around SAFE Terms

Chicago's investment community has developed distinct norms around SAFE negotiations that reflect the region's values and market dynamics.

Standard vs. Non-Standard Terms

Chicago investors generally prefer using Y Combinator's standard SAFE documents with minimal modifications. Founders who propose heavily negotiated or non-standard terms risk signaling inexperience or creating friction that delays closes.

Standard Chicago SAFE Structure:

  • Valuation cap appropriate for stage and traction
  • 15-20% discount
  • Pro rata rights for institutional investors ($250K+ checks)
  • MFN provisions
  • No board seat or governance rights
  • Standard information rights (quarterly updates, annual financials)

Terms Chicago Investors May Request:

  • Pro rata rights: Increasingly common for all investors, not just institutions
  • Side letters for strategic terms: Advisory roles, customer introductions, recruiting assistance
  • Reporting requirements: Monthly rather than quarterly updates for lead investors

Terms Founders Should Resist:

  • Super pro rata rights: Allowing investors to invest beyond their percentage in future rounds creates signaling problems and complicates later fundraising
  • Board seats or observer rights on SAFEs: Governance complexity at SAFE stage is premature and creates precedent problems
  • Blocking rights on future fundraising: Some investors request approval rights over future SAFE terms or priced rounds; founders should reject these provisions
  • Excessive discounts (25%+): High discounts signal investor concerns about company prospects or founder negotiating weakness

The "Party Round" Debate in Chicago

Party rounds—fundraising from numerous small investors without a clear lead—are viewed skeptically by Chicago's institutional investor community. While coastal markets sometimes celebrate "oversubscribed" party rounds as validation, Chicago investors interpret them as red flags:

  • Lack of Diligence: No lead investor means no one conducted comprehensive due diligence, increasing Series A risk
  • Coordination Challenges: Managing 15-30 small SAFE investors creates administrative burden and complicates future governance
  • Signaling Concerns: If no institutional investor would lead, what do they know that angels don't?

Chicago Best Practice: Raise from 3-8 investors with one clear lead (largest check, most engaged, strongest value-add). This structure provides capital while demonstrating institutional validation.

Common SAFE Mistakes Chicago Founders Make

Even with standard documents, founders make strategic and tactical errors that compromise future fundraising or create unnecessary dilution.

Mistake 1: Raising Too Much on SAFEs

Some founders continuously raise small SAFE tranches, accumulating $2M-$3M+ across multiple investors at various caps. This creates several problems:

  • Excessive Dilution: Multiple SAFEs at different caps compound dilution in ways founders often fail to model accurately
  • Series A Complications: Series A investors struggle to model cap tables with 5-6 SAFE tranches at different terms
  • Signaling Issues: Continuous fundraising suggests inability to achieve milestones or poor financial planning

Solution: Limit SAFE fundraising to 2-3 discrete rounds (pre-seed, bridge if needed, seed) with clear milestones justifying each raise. Plan to raise priced Series A once you reach $1M+ ARR and clear product-market fit.

Mistake 2: Setting Unrealistic Valuation Caps

Some founders reference San Francisco benchmarks to justify $8M-$10M pre-seed caps or $15M-$20M seed caps in Chicago. This strategy backfires:

  • Investor Rejection: Sophisticated Chicago investors pass on overpriced deals, leaving founders with only unsophisticated capital or no term sheet
  • Series A Crunch: High SAFE caps create high Series A valuation expectations that may be unachievable, triggering down rounds or inability to raise
  • Misaligned Investors: Investors who accept inflated caps often lack conviction and won't provide follow-on capital or strategic support

Solution: Use Chicago-specific benchmarks: $3.5M-$6M (pre-seed), $8M-$12M (seed). Accept that lower caps paired with lower burn rates often result in similar dilution to coastal equivalents with higher caps and higher burn.

Mistake 3: Negotiating Non-Standard Terms

Some founders, often influenced by startup podcasts or coastal fundraising stories, attempt to negotiate complex SAFE terms or create hybrid instruments. This approach rarely succeeds in Chicago:

  • Increased Legal Costs: Non-standard documents require attorney review, adding $3K-$8K in costs for both parties
  • Extended Timelines: Custom terms require negotiation cycles that delay closes by 3-6 weeks
  • Investor Skepticism: Chicago investors interpret creative structuring as warning sign about founder inexperience or difficult personalities

Solution: Use standard Y Combinator SAFE documents with standard terms. Save negotiating leverage for truly material terms like valuation cap, not esoteric provisions.

Mistake 4: Ignoring Cap Table Modeling

Many founders sign SAFEs without modeling dilution scenarios or understanding how multiple SAFE tranches interact at conversion.

Solution: Build detailed cap table models showing ownership after SAFE conversion under various Series A scenarios. Use tools like Carta or dedicated SAFE calculators to understand dilution before signing terms.

Mistake 5: Raising SAFEs Without Clear Milestones

Some founders raise SAFEs without articulating what metrics they'll achieve before Series A. This creates problems:

  • Inefficient Capital Deployment: Without clear milestones, founders spend capital on low-ROI activities
  • Series A Difficulty: Series A investors want to see deliberate progress against defined goals, not meandering experimentation
  • Investor Misalignment: SAFE investors expect their capital to fund specific achievements that derisk Series A

Solution: Before raising SAFEs, define 3-5 key milestones you'll achieve before Series A (revenue targets, customer logos, product development goals, team building). Communicate these to SAFE investors and report progress quarterly.

Strategic Considerations: When to Use SAFEs vs. Priced Rounds in Chicago

While SAFEs dominate Chicago pre-seed and seed fundraising, certain scenarios warrant priced equity rounds even at early stages.

When SAFEs Make Sense

  • Pre-Revenue or Minimal Revenue: Companies below $500K ARR typically lack metrics for rigorous valuation analysis
  • Fast Capital Needs: When you need to close capital quickly (4-6 weeks) to capture time-sensitive opportunities
  • Avoiding Governance Complexity: SAFEs defer board composition and governance negotiations until Series A when you have more leverage
  • Multiple Small Investors: Raising from 5-10 angels or small checks makes SAFEs administratively simpler than priced rounds
  • Uncertain Valuation Environment: During market volatility, SAFEs allow fundraising without committing to potentially unfavorable valuations

When Priced Rounds Make Sense

  • Strong Revenue Traction: Companies with $1M+ ARR and clear unit economics can command favorable priced terms
  • Strategic Investors Requiring Equity: Corporate VCs or strategic partners often require priced equity for accounting or governance reasons
  • Large Lead Investor: When raising $3M+ from a single institutional lead, priced rounds provide clarity on ownership and governance
  • Employee Liquidity: Priced rounds establish 409A valuations for stock option exercise, potentially enabling early employee liquidity
  • Cap Table Simplification: After multiple SAFE rounds, a priced round converts all SAFEs and establishes clear ownership structure
